The key that unlocks everything

First, a fiscal sponsor.

The club has no 501(c)(3), and every serious Maine funder requires one. The fix is a fiscal sponsor: a local nonprofit (library, adult education program, or friendly 501(c)(3)) that receives and disburses the funds on our behalf. One handshake, signed by September, unlocks five applications below.

  • An adult ed partner also satisfies the Davis Family Foundation education criteria. Double unlock.
  • A public library partner additionally unlocks the Stephen & Tabitha King Foundation Fall Grant: up to $50,000, due October 15, libraries only in 2026. The biggest partner‑routed check on the board.
  • MaineCF requires its own signed sponsorship form plus an advisory board of unrelated individuals. Start that paperwork early.

No sponsor, no fall applications. This is the whole ballgame before September.

Apply now, as we are

Four doors open today, no paperwork needed.

The Pollination Project

Up to $500, rolling daily. Funds informal, all‑volunteer groups with a community purpose, which is us exactly as we exist today. Apply here.

Emergent Ventures

Commonly $1,000 to $50,000+, rolling, thirty‑minute application, fast decisions. Funds individuals with scalable ideas. The pitch: a replicable template for rural AI‑literacy clubs, which is simply our funnel thesis told straight. Apply here.

Awesome Foundation

$1,000 monthly micro‑grants, no strings, anyone eligible. No Maine chapter; apply through a New England or thematic chapter. Apply here.

Kennebec Savings Bank

Community Dividends gives back nearly $1.5M locally each year through an always‑open portal. The small sponsorship channels have no posted nonprofit requirement; call the bank and ask about sponsoring a class series. A relationship worth having regardless. Start here.

The warm path

The Waterville family foundation.

The Bill & Joan Alfond Foundation gives $1.8M to $4.5M a year, favors Greater Waterville technology and innovation, and in 2020 funded a free community innovation course delivered through Bricks, Thomas College, and Tortoise Labs. Structurally, that program was almost exactly this club. There is no application; it is relationship money, and a warm path exists.

  • The right first ask is not cash. An introduction into their ecosystem (Dirigo Labs, Bricks, Thomas College) for space, sponsorship, or partnership captures most of the value with none of the spotlight.
  • Sequencing matters. Win one or two small neutral grants first, build a term of track record, and let this money fund the scale‑up rather than the birth. A famous benefactor at the founding moment reads as capture, and our whole brand is that commerce grows out of community.
  • The Harold Alfond Foundation (the big one) is closed to new applications until at least late 2027, requires a 501(c)(3), and favors track records. That timing matches ours: revisit with a year of receipts. Guidelines here.

Patience is the strategy. This door opens wider the longer we wait to knock.
